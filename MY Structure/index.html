What is farming?

Farming is growing food and raising animals to feed people and communities. Good farming needs healthy soil, clean water, and care for plants and animals.

  • Plant crops in the right season
  • Keep soil rich with compost
  • Protect animals from disease
  • Store harvest safely

Animal husbandry

Animal husbandry means raising and caring for farm animals — like cattle, goats, chickens, and pigs — for food, milk, eggs, or wool.

🐄 Cattle

Need grass, water, shade, and regular health checks.

🐐 Goats

Hardy animals — good for milk and meat in many areas.

🐔 Chickens

Provide eggs and meat. Keep coops clean and dry.

🐷 Pigs

Need strong fences, clean pens, and balanced feed.

Farming FAQ

Click a question to see the answer:

Compost is rotted plant and food waste mixed into soil. It adds nutrients and helps crops grow stronger.
Most farm animals need fresh water every day. Check troughs in the morning and evening, especially in hot weather.
Harvest when cobs are full and kernels feel hard. Dry cobs properly before storage to stop mould.
Crop rotation means changing what you plant in a field each season. For example, plant beans after maize — beans put nitrogen back in the soil and reduce pests.
Use strong wire mesh on the coop, lock doors at night, and remove leftover feed that attracts rats or wild animals. Check fences regularly for holes.
Weeds steal water, sunlight, and nutrients from your crops. Weed early and often so your maize, beans, or tomatoes grow faster and give a better harvest.
Dry grain fully before storage. Keep it in clean bags or bins in a cool, dry room. Check often for insects or mould and keep rodents out with tight containers.
Animal husbandry is the practice of raising and caring for farm animals — feeding, shelter, health checks, and breeding — so they stay healthy and productive.
